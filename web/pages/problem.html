1The problem

Definition

Fix a cost weight \(k > 0\) and a standard deviation \(\sigma > 0\). Let \(x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\) and \(w \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\) be independent real random variables. A controller pair consists of two Borel-measurable functions \(\gamma_1, \gamma_2 : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\), which act once each:

\[ u_1 = \gamma_1(x_0), \qquad x_1 = x_0 + u_1, \tag{1} \]
\[ y = x_1 + w, \qquad u_2 = \gamma_2(y), \qquad x_2 = x_1 - u_2. \tag{2} \]

The cost of a pair is

\[ J(\gamma_1,\gamma_2) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[\,k^2 u_1^2 + x_2^2\,\right], \tag{3} \]

and the problem is to determine the optimal cost

\[ J^\star(k,\sigma) \;=\; \inf_{\gamma_1,\,\gamma_2}\; J(\gamma_1,\gamma_2) \tag{4} \]

together with a pair that attains it.

Why it is hard

The second controller knows neither \(x_0\) nor \(u_1\), so it cannot reconstruct what the first controller knew. Under this non-classical information structure the action \(u_1\) does two jobs at once. It moves the state, which costs \(k^2 u_1^2\), and it shapes \(x_1\) so that \(x_1\) can be recognised through the noise \(w\), which is a form of signalling. If the second controller also saw \(x_0\), standard LQG theory would apply and linear controllers would be optimal.

Reduction to one function

Write \(f(x) = x + \gamma_1(x)\), so that \(x_1 = f(x_0)\). For a fixed \(f\), the second stage is a mean-square estimation problem, and the best \(\gamma_2\) is the conditional mean

\[ g_f(y) \;=\; \mathbb{E}[\,x_1 \mid y\,] \;=\; \frac{\displaystyle\int f(x)\,\varphi\big(y - f(x)\big)\,\varphi_\sigma(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}{\displaystyle\int \varphi\big(y - f(x)\big)\,\varphi_\sigma(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}, \tag{5} \]

where \(\varphi\) is the standard normal density and \(\varphi_\sigma(x) = \varphi(x/\sigma)/\sigma\). The problem therefore reduces to minimising a single functional,

\[ J(f) \;=\; k^2\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\big(f(x_0) - x_0\big)^2\right] \;+\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[\big(f(x_0) - g_f(y)\big)^2\right], \tag{6} \]

which is not convex in \(f\). Every cost on this site is \(J(f)\) as in (6), and a submission is a function \(f\).

What is known

  • An optimal pair exists for every \(k\) and \(\sigma\), and an optimal \(f\) satisfies \(\mathbb{E}[f(x_0)] = 0\) (Witsenhausen 1968, Theorem 1 and Lemma 9).
  • Among linear controllers \(f(x) = \lambda x\) the best cost is \(J_{\mathrm{lin}} = \min_{\lambda}\; k^2\sigma^2(1-\lambda)^2 + \lambda^2\sigma^2/(1+\lambda^2\sigma^2)\). For some parameters a nonlinear \(f\) does strictly better (Theorem 2). At the benchmark, \(J_{\mathrm{lin}} = 0.96\), while the two-point design \(f(x) = \sigma\,\mathrm{sgn}(x)\) costs \(0.4043\).
  • The optimal \(f\) is strictly increasing and has a real-analytic left inverse (Wu and Verdú 2011). No piecewise-linear \(f\) is therefore exactly optimal, although quantised and piecewise-linear controllers come arbitrarily close in cost (Saldi, Yüksel and Linder 2017).
  • Nearly all numerical work uses the benchmark \(k = 0.2\), \(\sigma = 5\). References and further results are listed in Section 5.

Lower bounds

Three lower bounds on \(J^\star\) hold for every controller pair, and the leaderboard lists their values for each setting, computed numerically and rounded down. With \((a)^+ = \max(a, 0)\),

\[ J^\star \;\ge\; \inf_{P \ge 0}\; k^2 P + \Big(\big(\sqrt{\kappa(P)} - \sqrt{P}\,\big)^{+}\Big)^{2}, \qquad \kappa(P) = \frac{\sigma^2}{(\sigma + \sqrt{P})^2 + 1}, \tag{7} \]
\[ J^\star \;\ge\; \inf_{P \ge 0}\; k^2 P + \sup_{\sigma_G \ge 1,\; L > 0}\; \frac{\sigma_G}{c(L)}\, e^{-L^2(\sigma_G^2 - 1)/2}\, \Big(\big(\sqrt{\kappa_2} - \sqrt{P}\,\big)^{+}\Big)^{2}, \tag{8} \]

where \(\kappa_2 = \sigma^2\sigma_G^2 \,\big/\, \big[c(L)^2\, e^{1 - d(L)} \big((\sigma + \sqrt{P})^2 + d(L)\,\sigma_G^2\big)\big]\), \(c(L) = 1/\Pr(|Z| \le L)\) and \(d(L) = \Pr(\lVert Z_3 \rVert \le L)\,/\Pr(|Z| \le L)\), with \(Z\) a standard normal variable and \(Z_3\) a standard normal vector in \(\mathbb{R}^3\). Finally,

\[ J^\star \;\ge\; \mathbb{E}\Big[\,\min_{a \ge 0}\; k^2\big(a - |x_0|\big)^2 + h(a)\Big], \qquad h(a) = a^2\,\mathbb{E}\big[\,1 - \tanh\!\big(a(a + w)\big)\big]. \tag{9} \]

Bounds (7) and (8) are Theorems 2 and 3 of Grover, Park and Sahai, the latter with vector length \(m = 1\). Bound (9) is Witsenhausen’s own lower bound, Theorem 3 of his 1968 paper: it tells the second controller \(|x_0|\) for free, which leaves it a two-point estimation problem. Wu and Verdú (2011, Theorem 4) generalise it.
